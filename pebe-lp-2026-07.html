From the manager

July was the stress test. The rules passed.

July was the most instructive month of the operating period. Not because it produced the highest income — June holds that record at $3,732 — but because it tested every rule the engine has, simultaneously, and every rule held.

The month opened with COIN rallying more than 15% in the first three weeks. For a covered-call engine, a violent upward move is the worst-case scenario — the written call gets blown through, forcing the manager to either accept capped gains at assignment or close the position at a loss to reposition. The engine chose the latter, twice. The COIN 167.50C was closed at a cost of $145 and immediately re-entered at the 170 strike for a cycle net credit of +$1,103. The COIN 170C ×2 was closed at a cost of $903 — the largest single-contract close of the operating period — and immediately re-entered at the 185 and 190 strikes for a cycle net credit of +$2,229. Both offensive rolls produced net credits. The rule — offensive rolls must always be net credit positive — was applied in real time under pressure and held.

At mid-month, July's realized P&L stood at −$174. The loss column showed three new entries. On a per-contract basis, the methodology correctly records these as negative outcomes. But calling them losses misrepresents what happened. They were the transaction cost of repositioning into larger premium during a favorable move — the exhaust from the engine running, not the engine failing. The roll cycle analysis below shows the full picture: July's roll cycles produced $3,332 in net credits against $1,048 in close costs.

The month's recovery came in the final week. COIN stabilized and the repositioned contracts decayed rapidly — the 185C was harvested at 79.3% capture ($752), the 190C ×2 at 83.5% capture ($1,823 — the largest single-position win of the entire operating period), and the SOFI 19C at 93.9% capture ($139). July closed at $2,691 realized — above the 6-month average, with every mid-month loss recovered and then some.

DAL entered the book this month as a UAL rotation within the airline/macro volatility cluster. UAL had produced repeat assignments — a signal under PEBE rules that the name may no longer meet entry criteria at current prices. DAL at $94.05 offered the same volatility profile at a lower price point. The first cycle (97.50C) was a clean $216 win at 86% capture. The stock then declined 10% to the mid-$80s, and the second cycle (87C) was a defensive roll to the 91C — a net debit of $76 to move the strike closer to cost basis. This is the same below-cost-basis discipline applied to SOFI earlier in the period. The rule is operating consistently across names.

SOFI resolved the cost-basis situation that dominated June reporting. Two contracts expired worthless (19.50C and 20.00C — both above the $19.00 cost basis), and the 19.00C was harvested at 93.9% capture. For the first time since May, all SOFI contracts are written at or above cost basis. The discipline rule — flag and roll any call written below cost basis — worked as designed, producing three consecutive applications across SOFI and DAL over a six-week period.

The operating period now stands at six consecutive positive months with no new capital added. The average monthly realized income of $2,557 has been generated across a VIX spike to 35, a broad market recovery, a sustained bull run, a crypto selloff, and a 15%+ single-name rally. One true loss in 51 closed contracts. The engine is not producing steady income — the monthly range spans $1,236 to $3,732 — but it is producing consistently positive income across fundamentally different conditions. That consistency is the signal, not any single month's number.

— The manager
