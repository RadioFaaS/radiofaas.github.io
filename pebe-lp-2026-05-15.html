What happened this week

This week's results reflect the engine operating under sustained upside pressure. The S&P 500 closed at all-time highs, the Nasdaq 100 continued its rally, and several of our underlying positions — most notably COIN — appreciated rapidly through our short-call strikes. We responded by rolling those contracts up: closing the original short call at a realized loss and writing a new contract at a higher strike. This is the protocol when an underlying moves faster than expected, and it preserves the position while capturing fresh premium at the new level.

The mechanics matter for reading the report. Five of the seven closed losses for the period are defensive rolls, not directional misses. They represent realized P&L on individual contracts that we deliberately closed in order to maintain our exposure and write at higher strikes. The trade-off was favorable: $1,328 in realized roll losses delivered $2,698 in fresh premium at the 220 strike level on COIN, a 2-to-1 ratio in the engine's favor.

May has now become the strongest premium month of the operating period at $2,653 captured in 15 days, exceeding the prior best month (April, $2,213). Open premium-in-flight has expanded from $2,778 last week to $4,000 across 7 active contracts. If even half of that captures, May and June will materially exceed the historical run-rate.

The engine has now operated through two distinct stress regimes. March 9, 2026: VIX peaks at 35.30 on a meaningful market drawdown — the engine took its largest cluster of called-away positions (3 contracts on AMD and UAL on April 17) inside that volatility window, and April still delivered $2,213 in premium. May 11–14, 2026: market melt-up — the engine rolled up 4 contracts on COIN in 3 days, retained the position, and ended the week with higher strikes and more premium-in-flight. The bounded-loss design held in both regimes. Maximum single-trade loss across the period: −$759 (0.9% of strategy capital).

Honest framing: the data here is suggestive, not conclusive. 3.5 months of operating data is enough to demonstrate process and survive two stress events, but not enough to project long-run performance with confidence. The track record will be built one reporting period at a time, and the rules of the engine will not change to chase outcomes. We expect normal-case performance to converge toward 22–28% annualized at scale, with strong months and weak months around that mean. The current 33.7% run-rate likely benefits from a constructive volatility environment that may not persist indefinitely.

— The manager

Methodology notes. Reporting period scope is Feb 1, 2026 onward — the post-tuning operating period of the engine. Pre-Feb activity is excluded as it reflects a different rule-set under development. All figures exclude positions outside the strategy mandate (long-term core holdings retained for non-strategic reasons). Net premium = total options income, realized + open. Average monthly premium = total premium from completed full months ÷ number of full months in scope. Annualized ROC = average monthly × 12 ÷ strategy capital base. Win rate counts closed contracts only; open contracts are not credited until closed. Defensive rolls (marked R) are realized losses on closed contracts where the original was repurchased and a new contract written at a higher strike to maintain the position. Benchmark comparisons use total return (price + dividends) for SPY and JEPQ vs premium income only for PEBE — a conservative framing that does not credit unrealized P&L on underlying positions. Capital base of $82,261 reflects strategy-active equity capital, excluding long-term holds. The strategy is currently operated by an individual; future scaling would require LP-appropriate fund structuring, which is in active design. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
